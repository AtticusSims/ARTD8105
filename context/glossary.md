*The course's working vocabulary. Use these words with these meanings.*

**Protocol** — a written procedure, specified precisely enough that someone else can carry it out without the author present. A LeWitt wall drawing certificate is one.

**Instruction** — the student's own written procedure. Versioned: `instruction_v1` was written by hand in session 1, `v2` after seeing it executed, `v3` optionally after the wall discussion.

**Parameter** — a decision the author has chosen to leave open, within bounds they set. Not a slider. The slider is how a parameter is operated.

**Underspecification audit** — reading an instruction for every phrase that could be executed more than one way, and naming the decision hiding inside each. The agent finds them; the student resolves them.

**The boring region** — where a different execution, or a different parameter value, would have changed nothing the author cared about. Not a flaw. It is slack, and it says what the piece is not about.

**The surprising region** — where a result stopped resembling what the author pictured but was still an honest execution of what they specified. The finding usually lives here.

**The breaking point** — where a result stops being a valid execution at all. If nothing could break it, the instruction is probably not saying anything.

**Variance** — the spread of different results from running the same procedure more than once. Interesting variance is not the same as noise, and telling them apart is a design judgement.

**Contribution type** — what kind of claim a paper makes: existence proof, method contribution, annotated portfolio, intermediate-level concept, critical or speculative, or empirical. Declared at the idea evaluation, and it determines what evidence is needed.

**Contestable** — a claim a reasonable person could disagree with. A claim nobody could dispute is a description in a claim's clothing.

**The four sentences** — *I made … / In making it I found that … / Which means that … / Which is useful to …* Sentence three is where papers are won and lost.

**Process journal** — a data collection instrument in which the student is the subject. Not a diary. Records decisions, rejected alternatives, failures, surprises, constraints and numbers.

**Disposition** — a record of the student turning down something the agent proposed, written at the moment. The hardest evidence to reconstruct later, and the most valuable.

**Venue file** — a reconstruction of a publication venue's tacit standards from public evidence. Built with the interrogation protocol in session 6.

**Derived standard** — what a venue appears to reward, measured from its own published papers rather than from its stated call.
