## Read this first

- **Any peer-reviewed publication counts for this course.** Choose a venue because your work fits it, not because of how it is indexed.
- **Choose the venue last.** Project, then contribution type, then format, then venue. A venue chosen first gets you writing to a call your work does not fit.
- **Everything here was checked on the venues' own pages on 6 October 2026.** Calls change. Before any date, word limit or format goes into your venue file, open the official page and check it yourself.
- **Deadlines are usually "Anywhere on Earth" (AoE).** An AoE deadline on a given day closes at **19:59 the following day in Macau**. SIGGRAPH uses 22:00 GMT, which is **06:00 the next morning in Macau**.
- **One piece of work, one venue at a time.** Sending closely related work to two venues at once can get both rejected.

---

## How publishing works in design research

- **A peer-reviewed conference paper is a full publication** in design and HCI, not a rehearsal for a journal. Most of the venues that suit this course are conferences.
- **Conferences** have one deadline a year, a decision in about two to three months, and a length set by the track. **Journals** take submissions at any time, and review takes months.
- **Archival or not.** A full paper or a pictorial is *archival*: it counts as the publication of that work, and the same work cannot be published again. Posters, works in progress and most art-track abstracts are *not archival*, so a full paper can follow later. That makes them a good first step for early work.
- **Showing a physical object.** Some tracks require you to bring or ship the object. An online-only conference can show it only as documentation — which a pictorial does well.

### What it costs

- **ACM venues are free for UM authors.** ACM has charged for open access since January 2026, but the [UM Library's agreement with ACM](https://um-mo.libguides.com/ta) covers UM staff and students with no quota — as long as the **corresponding author is at UM** and you **submit with your UM affiliation**. DIS, CHI, C&C, TEI and SIGGRAPH are ACM venues.
- **Most journals are free to publish in** on the ordinary subscription route. Open access is the optional extra that costs money.
- **Some journals have no fees at all:** *She Ji*, *Artnodes* and the *Journal of Science and Technology of the Arts*.
- **One charges after acceptance:** the *International Journal of Design*, US$1,000, with waivers possible.
- **Travel is the real cost** of a conference. CHI and SIGGRAPH are in the United States in 2027 — if you need a visa, allow time for it.

## Formats

**A pictorial and a short paper are different arguments, not the same argument at two lengths.** Choose the format before the venue.

| Format | Argues with | Typical length | Archival? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pictorial** | Images first; the text supports them | Up to 12 pages, excluding references | Yes — same weight as a full paper |
| **Full paper** | Prose first; images as evidence | About 5,000–8,000 words | Yes |
| **Art paper** | An artwork and the research behind it | 2,500–3,500 words, 5–10 images | Yes (SIGGRAPH) |
| **Artwork or exhibition track** | The object itself, shown | 2–6 pages, plus documentation and a technical rider | Usually not |
| **Poster, demo, work in progress** | Early results | 2–4 pages, plus a poster or video | No |
| **Journal article** | A sustained argument | 5,000–8,000 words | Yes |
| **Visual essay** (journal) | An argument made mainly through images | Up to about 12 pages | Yes |

**Look at the pictorial first.** It was created at DIS 2014 for exactly the kind of knowledge visual designers produce. In DIS's words, pictorials are "archival research publications with the same weight as full papers", in which visuals "play a major role in conveying the ideas and contributions" — and they "are meant to contribute to knowledge in their own right, not merely to document already-known concepts, methods, and processes." A portfolio with captions is not a pictorial.
