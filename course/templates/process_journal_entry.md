### Notes on keeping this journal

**This is a data collection instrument, and you are the subject.** It is not a
diary and it is not a progress report.

**Record:** decisions and the alternatives you turned down · failures in detail ·
surprises · constraints you discovered mid-process · the moment a project became
a different project · numbers.

**Do not record:** effort, mood, or narrative. *"I struggled with this for days"*
tells a reader nothing. *What* you struggled against, and what the resistance
revealed, tells them a great deal.

**Failures are the highest-value entries in the whole journal.** They reveal the
constraint structure of a material or tool, which is exactly the kind of
knowledge that transfers to someone who never sees your object. A term with no
recorded failures is a term with no findings.

**Write within 24 hours.** Memory smooths. It removes the false starts and
tidies the sequence into something that looks planned. Those false starts are
the evidence.

**One entry per event, not per day.** A day with three distinct failures is
three entries. A week of routine grinding is one.

### At session 10 you will analyse this

The procedure, so you can see now what you are collecting for:

1. Read the whole journal and mark every entry where `entry_type` is
   `failure`, `surprise` or `pivot`.
2. Group the marked entries. Recurring tensions appear — the same conflict in
   different clothes. Those groupings are candidate findings.
3. For each grouping, run the four-sentence test from `claim.md`. Most die at
   sentence 3.
4. What survives is your paper.

You cannot do this from memory, and you cannot do it from a journal that only
records what went well.
